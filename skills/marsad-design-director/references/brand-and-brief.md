# Brand evidence and asset choices

## Evidence order

1. Current explicit user instructions for this job, including exact copy, palette, typefaces, dimensions, and visual exclusions.
2. Supplied official brand guide, logos, typography and approved assets.
3. Approved examples from the same brand, with their campaign and date understood.
4. Reliable product/site context and factual references supplied or retrieved for the job.
5. Reversible decisions made for this one deliverable.

Do not infer a master brand rule from one campaign artwork. Do not transfer an old project's colors or type into a new product solely because both belong to Marsad Labs. Record which choices are confirmed and which are provisional; avoid describing provisional choices as approved.

## When the guide is incomplete

Use what is known. If the user provides five brand colors and two fonts, those are constraints; their exact values and font names matter. Within them, choose a composition and imagery that fit the message. If no assets or guide are supplied, derive a direction from the product, audience, platform and requested tone. Make useful work now. Ask for a logo file only when faithful placement of that specific logo is essential to the final export.

For a new sub-brand or product logo, distinguish parent-brand inheritance from product identity. A shared palette or attribution may carry family resemblance, while the mark itself can be structurally different. Do not invent an orange accent because a scheduling metaphor suggests a timestamp; use an off-palette color only with explicit authorization.

## Asset decision

| Need | Preferred action |
| --- | --- |
| Official logo/product/venue | Use the supplied original; preserve geometry and text |
| Realistic relevant scene not supplied | Find stock from a source with appropriate use rights, or generate a new image if suitable |
| Brand-specific scene no stock can represent | Generate a purpose-built visual and check its accuracy |
| Exact product UI, numbers or process | Construct with real screenshot, vectors, or typeset components |
| Illustration | Draw or generate a coherent original illustration as required |

Stock must fit the audience and message, not merely fill empty space. Verify use rights before delivering a commercial-ready asset. Do not fake a real venue, endorsement or product feature. Image generation is for visual assets when useful; put exact text, logos, data and product labels into reliable composition layers when possible. Inspect generated hands, objects, lighting and brand fidelity.

Do not assume all work needs photography, stock, or image generation. A type-led poster or precise diagram may be the stronger solution. The choice comes from content, not a template.
