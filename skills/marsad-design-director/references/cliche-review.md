# Contextual cliché check

Read this **after** choosing a direction or when critiquing an existing artifact. Do not paste this list into an image-generation prompt. It is a filter, not a visual style or provenance detector. Flag a pattern only when it weakens clarity, truth, originality, usability, or the actual brand.

- **Interchangeability:** Could the logo, brand name or product be swapped without rethinking the composition or imagery?
- **Default styling:** Are gradient, glow, glass, bevel, grain, neon, beige editorial, dark gold, or hard-border effects doing the job of an idea?
- **Repetitive layout:** Does this asset reuse a recent composition even though its message or product differs? Compare actual examples when available.
- **Symbol convergence:** For logo options, are the silhouettes, primitives, stroke patterns, accent marks or lockups nearly identical across different concepts?
- **Decoration overload:** Do arrows, orbs, grids, badges, pseudo-data, tiny labels or shadows lack an informational or brand role?
- **Generic type:** Was the typeface chosen because it is common in generated designs rather than because it serves the brand and language? A common font is acceptable when justified.
- **Generic imagery:** Do plastic-looking people, arbitrary product cutouts, impossible hands, implausible reflections or fake interfaces weaken trust?
- **Empty copy:** Does text promise “elevation,” “the future,” or transformation without a specific point the artwork supports?
- **Template drift:** Is a known trend substituted for an older AI trend while the same generic structure remains?

For each real issue: point to the exact element, say what it prevents the viewer from understanding, and revise that element. Do not ban a color, font, circle or gradient globally. If a brand owns that device or the content calls for it, use it deliberately.

The examples supplied by the user reveal two regression checks: a caption crossing a central connector/shape fails the alignment review; multiple distinct product logos sharing the same route-line-plus-small-accent construction fail the concept distinctness review. These are examples of failure modes, not forbidden shapes.
